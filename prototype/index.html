    REMY · PRODUCT PLAN · V0.1

    A personal chef for one prep day a week

    Remy learns what one person genuinely enjoys eating, then turns it into a realistic week: a seven-day plan, one consolidated grocery list, and a timed prep session that fits the time they actually have.

    Value proposition

    For a picky eater who loves sweets and cooks once a week, Remy plans meals they will actually eat, with sweets built in, so they can lose weight sustainably without a plan that falls apart by Wednesday.

    Most meal planners start from “healthy recipes” and hope the person adapts. Remy starts from the person: accepted foods, textures, preparations, sauces and cravings. It then applies safety rules and practical limits (time, freezer space, budget) with rules that work without AI.

    What Remy is not

    • Not a dietitian or medical advice. Remy gives general balance guidance (protein at meals, fruit and vegetables daily, sensible portions). It doesn’t diagnose, prescribe diets, or promise results. Personal targets come from a professional, and Remy can plan around them.
    • Not a calorie tracker. Calorie estimates are optional and always labeled as estimates.
    • Not a restriction engine. Sweets are planned, portioned and scheduled; they are never earned or banned.

    Target user and problems

    Version 1 is designed for one person: an experienced home cook with no software background, who is a picky eater, loves sweets, has one day a week for cooking, uses both a phone and a computer, and wants the app to be free to start.

    ProblemWhat happens todayHow Remy responds
    Plans ignore tasteRecipes feature foods they won’t eat; the plan is abandoned.Six-level food scale, accepted preparations, texture rules. Only foods rated Okay or better are used.
    Sweets are bannedCravings build until the plan breaks.A daily or near-daily sweet slot, pre-portioned, with a mix of real and lighter desserts.
    One day to cookFood goes bad by day five, or prep takes all day.A scheduler that fits the time window, and fridge or freezer assignment by day.
    Boredom vs. effortVariety means more recipes and longer prep.A variety control that shows its effect on prep time before committing.
    Grocery chaosDuplicates, forgotten items, buying what’s already home.One consolidated list by store section, with pantry items removed.

    Complete user flow

    Welcome→ Conversational interview→ Profile summary→ Confirm→ 7-day plan→ Approve / replace / move→ Grocery list→ Prep-day timeline→ Eat through the week→ Weekly check-in→ Next week’s plan

    The last step loops back to planning. The interview can be paused at any point (saved after every answer) and resumed from the welcome screen or the progress screen. Any answer can be edited later from the profile or preferences.

    MVP acceptance criteria

    Each feature is done when every statement in its row is true on a phone and on a computer.

    FeatureAcceptance criteria
    Conversational interviewExactly one question is visible at a time. Follow-up questions appear only when their condition is met (for example, the allergy confirmation appears only if an allergy is selected). Nonessential questions show Skip; safety questions don’t. Quick answers, multi-select and free text all work. A tester using example answers finishes in under 10 minutes.
    Save and resumeEvery answer is saved within 1 second. Closing the app and reopening it shows a resume option that returns to the same question. Progress by section is visible.
    Change answersBack returns to the previous question with the old answer selected. Changing an answer re-evaluates follow-ups: new ones are asked; answers from branches that no longer apply are ignored by the profile.
    Profile summaryShows goals, hard restrictions, the six food levels with preparations, flavors, textures, sweets, variety, cooking day, equipment, storage, budget, schedule, inferences and unanswered questions. Each conclusion shows a confidence level. No plan is generated until the user confirms.
    Hard restrictionsNo recipe, sauce or substitution containing a restricted ingredient or a known alias is ever shown as available. Blocked options are labeled with the reason. Changing a restriction requires an explicit confirmation step. The automated allergy test suite reports zero violations.
    Weekly plannerCovers 7 days for the meals the user chose. Each meal can be approved, rejected (reason optional, one tap), replaced or moved. Replacing one meal changes no other meal. Changing variety keeps approved meals.
    Storage safetyEvery planned portion is labeled fridge or freezer. No cooked meal is scheduled past its fridge limit unless it is frozen. Moves that would break this are disabled with a reason. Frozen meals that need thawing produce a reminder the evening before.
    Recipe detailsShows why it was chosen, ingredients, steps, fridge and freezer limits, thawing and reheating to 165°F / 74°C. Substitutions include only foods rated Okay or better that pass the restriction check.
    Grocery listIdentical ingredients are merged with summed quantities. Items are grouped by store section. Items can be checked, edited, deleted and added. Pantry items appear under “Already at home” and can be moved back. Costs appear only when the currency has price data, and are shown as a range.
    Prep timelineTasks are in time order with lane labels (hands-on, oven, stove, chill). Overlapping tasks are visible. The total is compared with the user’s window; if it runs over, the app says by how much and offers a fix. Container, bag and freezer counts are shown.
    Balance checksEach day shows whether every main meal meets the protein guide and whether fruit and vegetables reach 3 servings. “Balance this day” adds only sides that pass the restriction check, match accepted foods and preparations, and are storage-safe for that day. Every side can be removed. With “No thanks”, no flags appear.
    Estimates and targetsCalorie and protein numbers are hidden unless the user chose detailed guidance or turns them on, and appear as ranges. Days estimated under about 1,200 kcal are flagged, never planned deliberately. Professional targets can be entered and are compared, not enforced.
    Weekly feedbackEvery recipe from the week can be rated in under 2 minutes. The optional body check-in (hunger, energy, fit, weekly weight) is saved to progress. Reasons are optional. The screen lists the changes Remy will make before saving.
    Preferences editorEach learned item shows its source and confidence and can be deleted. Any food can be moved to another level. “Delete everything” removes local and cloud data after an in-app confirmation.
    PWA and syncInstalls on iPhone, Android and desktop. The plan, recipes and grocery list open offline. A change on one device appears on the other within 1 minute when both are online.

    AI inputs and structured outputs

    The MVP runs without AI. When AI is added, it works inside strict boundaries: the rules engine prepares its inputs, and every output is checked by the same rules before the user sees it.

    FeatureRule-based (no AI, free)Needs an AI model
    InterviewBranching, skip, resume, fixed-choice answersUnderstanding free-text answers (“I only eat broccoli if it’s crunchy”)
    SafetyRestriction filter, alias dictionary, storage limitsNever. AI can’t change or override these.
    PlanningTemplates, scoring by ratings, replacement rankingNew recipe ideas, adapting a recipe to a preparation
    Groceries and prepConsolidation, sections, scheduling, container countsNone needed
    LearningRating weights, confidence, decaySummarizing free-text feedback into suggested preference changes
    ConversationScripted Remy linesOpen chat about a meal or a swap

    Inputs sent to the model

    • The profile, without name or email: food levels with preparations, flavors, textures, sweets, schedule, equipment, time window.
    • Hard restrictions, as read-only constraints.
    • A candidate recipe list that the rules have already filtered.
    • The last four weeks of ratings and rejection reasons.

    Structured output (example: a replacement)

    {
  "type": "meal_replacement",
  "day": "Tue", "slot": "Lunch",
  "recipe_id": "bbqbowl",
  "reason": "Uses chicken and rice (Love) with BBQ, one of your sauces",
  "uses_accepted_foods": ["chicken", "rice", "cheese"],
  "storage": { "method": "fridge", "day_after_prep": 2 },
  "confidence": "medium"
}

    Outputs are requested with a JSON schema so the app can validate them. If validation fails, or the result touches a restricted ingredient, the app retries once and then falls back to the rule-based choice.

    Preference-learning strategy

    Remy keeps learning after onboarding, but only from signals the user can see, and it asks before making a big change.

    SignalEffectConfidence
    Interview answerSets the food level or preference directlyHigh
    “Loved it” ratingRecipe +2; its main foods get a small boostHigh
    “Not again” + reasonRecipe −4; the reason adjusts related rules (texture, effort, reheating)High
    Rejected in planner with reasonRecipe −2Low
    Replaced without a reasonRecipe −1Low
    Same food rejected twiceA one-question follow-up instead of an assumptionAsks first
    Inference from several answersShown as “What I inferred”Medium or low
    • Behavioral signals fade over about 8 weeks. Direct answers don’t fade.
    • Remy never moves a food to “Never suggest” by itself.
    • Every learned item shows where it came from and can be deleted. Deleting it also reverses its effect.
    • If the user wants to try new foods, Remy suggests at most one small, low-risk item a week, in a preparation they already like.

    Nutrition layer

    Remy helps the user eat in a balanced way that supports sustainable fat loss, using only foods they accept. It works like a careful cook who knows the basics of a balanced plate, not like a dietitian.

    CheckRule in version 1How Remy fixes it
    Protein at mealsAbout 15 g+ at breakfast, 25 g+ at lunch and dinner. Raised by 5 g if the user reports being often hungry.Suggests a protein side the user accepts, such as a sweetened Greek yogurt cup or scrambled eggs
    Fruit and vegetablesAt least 3 servings a day (5 is great). Vegetables hidden in sauces count.Adds a produce side in the user’s accepted form: crispy broccoli, air-fried green beans, berries
    PortionsA plate guide (half fruit and veg, a quarter protein, a quarter starch), applied to prep-day containers where the recipe allowsEach recipe states its portion in plain terms (“one jar”, “¾ cup rice”)
    Light daysFlags any day estimated under about 1,200 kcal, a commonly used minimum for unsupervised plansSuggests a snack or side; never plans lower on its own
    SweetsAlways planned and portioned; never counted against the user—
    • User control. During the interview the user chooses “suggest sides”, “just show me” or “no thanks”. Every added side can be removed.
    • Estimates are optional. Calorie and protein numbers are hidden unless the user asked for detailed guidance or turns them on, and they’re shown as ranges.
    • Professional targets. The user can enter targets from a dietitian or doctor. Remy shows how the week compares; it doesn’t change targets or push lower.
    • Progress without a scale. Weekly check-in on hunger, energy and how clothes fit. Weight is optional, weekly, and shown only as a multi-week trend.
    • Nutrition data. The prototype uses rough estimates. The real app would use USDA FoodData Central (a free government database with a free API key) and Open Food Facts for branded products.
    Remy doesn’t calculate calorie needs from body data, handle medical conditions, or respond to signs of disordered eating beyond pointing to professional help. Those need a qualified person.

    Allergy and food-safety safeguards

    Allergies are constraints, not preferences. They are stored in their own table, confirmed during the interview, applied before and after any AI step, and can only be changed by the user through a confirmation screen.

    Allergy safeguards

    • Alias dictionary. Each allergen maps to known ingredient names, for example shellfish → shrimp, crab, lobster, oyster sauce. The prototype blocks a beef-and-broccoli recipe because of its oyster sauce.
    • Two checks. The rules filter candidates before planning and re-check the finished plan, including substitutions.
    • Visible blocking. A blocked option says why instead of silently disappearing, so the user can trust the filter.
    • Label reminder. Brands differ. Remy reminds users to check labels, and can avoid “may contain” products when asked.
    • No medical claims. Health conditions are optional, and Remy recommends professional guidance instead of tailoring plans medically.

    Storage rules the scheduler enforces

    Based on common food-safety guidance such as the USDA’s: refrigerate cooked food within 2 hours; keep the fridge at 40°F / 4°C or below; eat cooked leftovers within 3–4 days; keep the freezer at 0°F / −18°C (frozen food stays safe, with best quality in roughly 2–3 months); thaw in the fridge, cold water or microwave; reheat to 165°F / 74°C. Anything planned past its fridge limit is moved to the freezer on prep day, or flagged if it doesn’t freeze well.

    Privacy and data-deletion controls

    • Collect less. No weight, measurements or calorie target is required. Health information is optional and can be “prefer not to say”.
    • Local first. Data lives on the device and syncs only after sign-in. The app keeps working if the cloud is unavailable.
    • Minimal AI payloads. No name or email is sent to an AI provider. Before enabling AI, the app explains which provider is used and how that provider handles data.
    • User controls. Delete a single learned preference, delete all learned preferences, export everything, or delete everything (device and cloud) with one confirmation.
    • No selling, no ads. State this in a one-page privacy note written in plain language.

    Progressive Web App strategy

    • Manifest. Name “Remy”, basil-green theme color, icons at 192 and 512 px, standalone display.
    • Offline. A service worker caches the app itself and the current week: plan, recipes, grocery list and prep timeline. Grocery check-offs made offline sync later.
    • Install. Android and desktop Chrome show an install prompt. On iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen. Show a short in-app guide for each.
    • Storage. Ask the browser to keep data persistent so it isn’t cleared automatically.
    • Reminders (release 2). Web push for “move the sauce to the fridge tonight”. On iPhone this works only after installing to the home screen.

    Terms, explained

    PWA (Progressive Web App): a website that can be installed like an app, works offline, and runs on phones and computers.
    API: a way for one program to ask another for something, like the app asking an AI model for a recipe idea.
    Free tier: the amount of a paid service you can use at no charge. It has limits and can change.
    Local storage / IndexedDB: storage inside the browser on one device. Fast and private, but not shared between devices.
    Sync: keeping the same data on several devices by storing a copy in the cloud.
    Server function: a small piece of code that runs in the cloud, used here to keep the AI key secret.
    JSON schema: a description of the exact shape a piece of data must have, so the app can reject malformed AI answers.
    Rules engine: ordinary code with fixed rules (like “never include shellfish”) that gives the same answer every time.
